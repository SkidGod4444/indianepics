J. P. Joshi found the site in 1967-68 and R. S. Bisht excavated it from 1990 to 2005. Inside its walls lay a citadel, a middle town and a lower town, and around them a chain of reservoirs cut into the rock and fed by two seasonal streams, the Manhar and the Mansar: water engineering in one of the driest corners of the Harappan world. UNESCO dates the occupation to about 3000 to 1500 BCE; the range shown is the mature urban phase.

On the map it marks the southern reach of the Harappan cities, near the Gulf of Kutch and the sea routes to Oman and Mesopotamia. The signboard, ten signs inlaid in gypsum and found fallen near the north gate, is the largest Indus inscription known. Like every other, it is unread.
