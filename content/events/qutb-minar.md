The mosque now called Quwwat al-Islam was raised in the 1190s inside the old Chahamana citadel, and its own inscription says it was built from the materials of twenty-seven temples. The minar beside it went up in stages: Aibak built the first storey, Iltutmish added three, and Firuz Shah Tughluq rebuilt the top after lightning struck it in 1368. It stands 72.5 metres. In the courtyard is an iron pillar of the Gupta period, brought here from elsewhere and some eight hundred years older than the mosque.

When Muhammad of Ghur was killed in 1206 his Indian commanders divided his conquests. Aibak ruled from Lahore; it was Iltutmish, from about 1211, who made Delhi the seat of an independent sultanate. Its five dynasties built a succession of cities nearby, Siri, Tughlaqabad, Jahanpanah and Firozabad, which is why the medieval capital is a scatter of ruins across south Delhi.

Historians disagree over how to read the reused temple stone: as a deliberate display of conquest, as builders' practical recycling, or as both at once.
