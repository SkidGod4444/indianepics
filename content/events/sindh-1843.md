Sindh's Talpur amirs had been made to let British armies cross their country to Afghanistan in 1838-39. After the disaster at Kabul, Charles Napier, sent to impose a harsher treaty, provoked a war. On 17 February 1843 his force of about 2,800 broke a far larger Baluch army at Miani, and on 24 March it won again at Dubba; both fields lie a few miles from Hyderabad, where the pin stands. Sindh was annexed, governed by Napier until 1847, and then attached to the Bombay Presidency.

The conquest gave the Company the lower Indus and the port of Karachi, which grew into the main outlet of the Punjab after 1849.

Even contemporaries thought the war unjust; Napier's own journal called the seizure a piece of rascality, if a useful one. The one-word dispatch "Peccavi" ("I have sinned") attributed to him was a joke printed in Punch.
