The Kushans came from Bactria, north of the Hindu Kush, and under Kanishka held both Purushapura, modern Peshawar, and Mathura. The Rabatak inscription, found in Afghanistan in 1993, has Kanishka claim cities as far east as Saketa, Kaushambi and Pataliputra. His coins put Greek, Iranian and Indian gods side by side, the Buddha among them, and a headless statue of him in a long Central Asian coat was found at Mat, north of Mathura.

The red sandstone workshops of Mathura and the grey schist carvers of Gandhara produced, in these decades, some of the earliest images of the Buddha in human form; which school came first is still argued. So was Kanishka's date, debated for a century between 78 and 278 CE, until Harry Falk's reading of an astronomical text in 2001 put the start of his era at 127 CE. Most historians now accept it, and the extent of his rule in the Ganga plain remains a matter of how far to trust Rabatak.
