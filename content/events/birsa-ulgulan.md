The Munda villages of Chota Nagpur held land as khuntkatti, cleared and owned by the founding clan. Through the nineteenth century outside landlords, moneylenders and contractors, the dikus, turned the Mundas into tenants owing rent and forced labour, and the courts and missions offered little redress. Birsa, born at Ulihatu around 1875 and schooled at a German mission, began preaching in the 1890s as a prophet of a reformed faith and of Munda rule, and his followers called him Dharti Aba, father of the earth.

The ulgulan, the great tumult, began on Christmas Eve 1899 with attacks on churches, police posts and landlords' agents around Khunti. On 9 January 1900 troops fired on Birsa's followers gathered on Dombari Buru, a hill above Sail Rakab near Ulihatu; the number killed is not known, and Munda memory puts it far above the official figure. Birsa was captured in the Jamkopai forest on 3 February and died in Ranchi jail on 9 June 1900, officially of cholera. The pin marks his birthplace.

The Chota Nagpur Tenancy Act of 1908 recognised khuntkatti rights and restricted the sale of tribal land to outsiders, and it is still in force. Jharkhand was created on 15 November 2000, Birsa's birth anniversary, which since 2021 is also observed nationally as Janjatiya Gaurav Divas.
