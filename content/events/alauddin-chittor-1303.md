Alauddin marched from Delhi at the end of January 1303 and entered the fort on 26 August. Amir Khusrau, who was with the army, says the Sultan ordered some 30,000 people killed and renamed the fort Khizrabad after his son. In the same years Alauddin threw back repeated Mongol invasions of the Punjab and the Delhi region, and fixed grain and market prices in the capital to pay for a large standing army.

His general Malik Kafur then took Delhi's armies further south than any before them: to Devagiri, to the Kakatiya capital Warangal in 1310, and into the Hoysala and Pandya countries in 1311. These campaigns brought tribute, not direct rule, but they opened the way for the Tughluq annexations of the 1320s.

The best-known story of Chittor, that Alauddin besieged it to seize Queen Padmini and that the fort's women burned themselves rather than be taken, is first told in Malik Muhammad Jayasi's poem Padmavat of 1540. Khusrau, Barani and Isami, writing within decades of the siege, do not mention her.
