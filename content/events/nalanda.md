Xuanzang, who studied here in the 630s, was told that the monastery was begun by a king Shakraditya, and finds from the site support identifying him with the Gupta emperor Kumaragupta I, who ruled from about 415 to 455. Later Gupta kings, Harsha and the Pala kings of Bengal added monasteries, temples and the revenues of villages; the excavated ruins are a row of brick monasteries facing a row of temples.

Nalanda taught Buddhist philosophy, logic, grammar and medicine, and its teachers shaped the Buddhism of Tibet. It declined as royal patronage moved to the Pala foundations at Vikramashila and Odantapuri, and was sacked around 1200; the usual attribution to Bakhtiyar Khalji's troops rests on later sources, and a Tibetan pilgrim found a few monks still teaching in the ruins in the 1230s. Alexander Cunningham identified the site in the 1860s.
