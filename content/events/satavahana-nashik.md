The Satavahanas ruled the western Deccan from Pratishthana, modern Paithan, and grew rich on the trade between the Ganga plain, the Deccan and the ports of the west coast. In the early second century the Kshaharata Shaka ruler Nahapana held the coast and Nashik. Gautamiputra Satakarni defeated him and restruck his silver coins in his own name; a hoard found at Jogalthembi near Nashik in 1906, most of it restruck, shows it happened.

The inscription in Cave 3 at Nashik, set up by his mother Gautami Balashri in the nineteenth regnal year of her grandson Vasishthiputra Pulumavi, calls Gautamiputra the destroyer of Shakas, Yavanas and Pahlavas and lists the regions he ruled. The Satavahana chronology is disputed: a long chronology begins the dynasty in the third century BCE, a short one in the first, and Gautamiputra's reign is placed anywhere from about 86 to 130 CE.
