After the Mauryas, power in the subcontinent was plural and regional: the Satavahanas in the Deccan; Indo-Greeks, Shakas and then the Kushans in the north-west; the Chera, Chola and Pandya kings of the Tamil country. From about 320 CE the Guptas built the largest state of the period across the Ganga plain. Their era produced Kalidasa's court poetry, the Ajanta paintings, Aryabhata's astronomy and the monastery at Nalanda. After them came Harsha's Kannauj in the north and the Chalukyas and Pallavas in the south.

The outline is the Gupta empire at its widest, under Chandragupta II around 400 to 415 CE, when the defeat of the western Kshatrapas brought Malwa and Gujarat under Gupta rule. It is approximate. The Allahabad pillar describes rings of kings who were uprooted, captured and released, or paid tribute, not a border; the line drawn here is the zone of direct rule and leaves the tributaries outside.
