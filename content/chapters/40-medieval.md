From the eighth century no single power held the north for long. The Gurjara-Pratiharas, Palas and Rashtrakutas fought over Kannauj, while in the south the Cholas built a state that reached across the sea to Sri Lanka and Southeast Asia. Armies from Ghazni raided the plains in the eleventh century, and after Tarain in 1192 Ghurid commanders founded a sultanate at Delhi that outlasted five dynasties.

Under the Khaljis and Muhammad bin Tughluq the Sultanate reached further south than any northern state since the Mauryas, then lost the Deccan to the Bahmanis and the far south to Vijayanagara within a generation. Timur's sack of Delhi in 1398 left it a regional kingdom, which the Lodis held until Babur.

The outline is the Delhi Sultanate around 1330, at its greatest reach. It is the least certain shape on this site: in Ma'bar the Sultan's writ lasted barely a dozen years, in the Deccan under thirty, and Bengal was independent by 1338.
